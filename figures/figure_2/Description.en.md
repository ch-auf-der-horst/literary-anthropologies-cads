# Figure 2 · Justifications of human authorship

The figure brings together selected arguments from manuscript sections 4–7. Its three upper fields concern a work’s personal history, work on the text and public attribution. Connectors indicate analytical relationships, not quantities or a chronological production sequence.

Delabar, Li Shaojun and BT-DE-A-022 ground the value of human participation in a work’s history, lived experience and personal development of ideas. Bauer attributes a successful metaphor to the stimulus provided by his own experimental arrangement.

The central fork asks which form of participation counts as creative. Xu Junrui links her procedure to her conception of the project and her personal judgment that it is complete; Tischer describes prompting and editing. Zhang Minghui interprets the role of selecting as a distancing from creation. Two comments on Tischer’s experiment challenge its justification on different grounds. This comparison does not assume identical workflows for Xu and Zhang.

The right-hand column identifies different forms of attribution and accountability. Tischer’s author field organises publication; Heß calls for a person behind literary judgment; and BT-EN-A-001 accepts accountability for commissioned illustrations within a book production. These cases connect personal accountability to different activities, without presupposing the same creative achievement.

The shared finding is that creative status and attribution to a person can diverge. Disclosure makes participation open to scrutiny but does not guarantee recognition. This is an interpretative synthesis of the identified cases, rather than a comprehensive typology or a measure of economic erosion.

## Reading the German labels

`Erfahrung` means experience; `Arbeit am Text` means work on the text; `öffentliche Zurechnung` means public attribution; `schöpferisch` means creative; and `personale Zurechnung` means attribution to a person. Case IDs refer to the [case index](../../data/belegstellen_v12.csv), whose fields are explained in the [English data guide](../../data/README.md).
